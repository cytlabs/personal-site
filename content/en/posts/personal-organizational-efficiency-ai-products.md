In AI projects, I often see three things confused: **personal productivity, organizational productivity and building AI products.** They relate to one another but solve different problems.

A client recently asked: “Didn't you say my workflow had little room left for efficiency improvement? Why do we still need this platform?”

It is a good question. Without separating the three, it seems contradictory: if a person is already efficient, why use more AI or build a platform?

The key question is: **what do we want AI to change?**

## Personal productivity: give yourself better tools

Imagine a chef who takes ten minutes to prepare a dish. Better tools and methods reduce it to six minutes and improve the result.

The restaurant, other people and the wider business have not changed. **The chef has become more capable.**

AI's first layer of value often works this way. Preparing a report once required research, reading, organization, analysis, writing and revision. Now you can ask AI to analyze materials, identify key issues, research them further and prepare a report. A two- or three-hour task might take half an hour.

Personal productivity often does not require a new platform. Existing tools such as ChatGPT, Claude and Codex are powerful; learning to work with them matters more.

Understand their strengths and weaknesses, communicate goals and context, decide what to delegate and what to judge yourself, and capture recurring methods as prompts, skills, knowledge or workflows.

The shift is from doing it alone to doing it with AI. The core is **giving one person stronger tools so they become more capable.**

## Organizational productivity: redesign how everyone works

The chef is faster, but customers still wait half an hour. Orders move from servers to the kitchen, completed dishes need coordination, staff ask which table they belong to, and managers spend time resolving confusion.

**A faster chef does not automatically make a faster restaurant.** Reducing six minutes to five may matter less than asking why information passes through so many people, which work needs people, which machines can handle and which steps can disappear.

A redesigned flow might be:

**Customer orders → order enters the kitchen automatically → work is assigned → completion triggers notification → only exceptions reach the manager.**

This changes how the restaurant operates rather than merely improving everyone's tools.

Companies often give sales, operations, project managers and HR separate AI tools. That helps. But if the process still involves A organizing materials for B, B explaining to C in a meeting, C executing, B checking and A reconfirming, making each person 30% faster can leave the overall process inefficient.

Ask: **if we designed the company from scratch today, would we still work this way?**

Previously, a client meeting might require an assistant's notes, a manager's interpretation, another team meeting and continuous manual follow-up. With AI, a possible design is a meeting that creates project context, decomposes tasks, supports direct team execution and ongoing follow-up, routing key decisions to the manager.

The change is not merely AI writing meeting notes. Work previously dependent on repeated human transfer and coordination has been redesigned.

The core is **redesigning how people and AI work together.** A highly efficient individual does not imply an efficient organization.

## AI products: redesign how a problem is solved

In the restaurant analogy, better kitchen tools represent personal productivity; redesigned operations represent organizational productivity. The third activity might be building Meituan, a food-ordering and delivery platform.

Its purpose is not making a chef cook faster. It changes how users find restaurants, order, pay and receive meals: part of how the problem of eating is solved.

Consider education. A teacher using AI for lesson preparation is improving personal productivity. A school assigning daily questions, homework feedback and progress tracking to AI while teachers focus on lesson design and important guidance is changing organizational work.

A product offering every student a persistent AI teacher that understands their knowledge, history, weaknesses and goals and adapts instruction individually addresses another problem: **how can personalized one-to-one teaching become affordable?**

Previously, that service was difficult to scale, not merely inefficient. AI makes another solution possible.

Start product design by asking which problem is being solved and whether AI makes a previously unavailable approach possible, rather than asking how to improve efficiency.

## AI products need not be about efficiency

AI can turn two hours into twenty minutes. It can also make expensive services broadly available, personalize standardized products, let software act on understood goals instead of waiting for clicks, and create new experiences.

Evaluation should ask not only how much time is saved, but whether the product offers a solution that did not exist before.

## How the three differ

| Dimension | Personal productivity | Organizational productivity | AI products |
|---|---|---|---|
| What changes | One person's capability | An organization's operation | A problem's solution |
| Central question | How can I work better with AI? | Should we still collaborate this way? | How else can this problem be solved? |
| Typical approach | Use existing AI tools | Redesign processes, roles and responsibilities | Create products and experiences |
| Does it require a platform? | No | Not necessarily | Usually needs a product form |
| Outcome | A more capable individual | A different way of working | A new solution |

Personal productivity means better tools for one person. Organizational productivity means redesigning human–AI work. An AI product means redesigning the solution to a problem.

## How to approach each

### For personal productivity, focus on learning

Learn to use AI, provide context, collaborate with agents, judge results and capture reusable methods before developing a system. Build your own way of working with AI.

### For organizational productivity, understand the business again

Before buying tools, follow employees through actual work. Trace origins, participants, information, judgement, waiting, rework and mere information transfer.

Then decide what people do, what AI does, what they do together and what no longer needs doing. Test the new working method before deciding whether it needs a system.

### For AI products, focus on the problem

Do not collect agents, RAG, memory and MCP in a platform simply because they are popular. Ask who uses it, what problem they face, how they solve it now, why that falls short and what AI changes: speed, price, quality or a new capability.

Then select agents, memory, skills, knowledge bases, workflows or other technology. **Technology should grow from the problem, not seek a problem afterward.**

## They can reinforce each other without being the same

An employee adopting AI may improve individual productivity. A company may then reconsider approvals, reporting, handoffs and roles. A useful organizational approach may eventually become a product for other companies.

But these are not mandatory sequential stages. Someone can use Codex without changing an organization. A company can reorganize with existing tools without building a platform. A founder can create a consumer AI product unrelated to organizational efficiency.

They remain three distinct problems.

## A final thought

An easy mistake is seeing AI and immediately imagining features. First ask what you are changing.

If it is individual capability, study personal collaboration with AI. If it is organizational operation, reconsider people, AI, roles and processes. If it is a product, return to users and problems and ask whether AI enables a better solution.

**AI is not the purpose.** It lets us reconsider how one person works, how people work together and how familiar problems should be solved. The interesting work may only be beginning.
