## From finding people to obtaining capabilities

## How AI might reshape recruiting

## Before we start

I do not work in the recruiting industry. Reading product documentation raised a question: will AI merely accelerate recruiting, or redefine it?

If it only improves efficiency, HR teams, platforms, headhunters and their specialized roles remain, with each person producing more. If it changes information, search, evaluation and coordination costs, roles and business models may change too.

## What problem does recruiting really solve?

At the surface, recruiting helps companies find suitable people. Employers post roles, applicants submit resumes, platforms connect them, HR advances the process and headhunters seek scarce talent.

But completing recruitment is not the company's ultimate need. A role exists because work must be done, a problem solved or a capability established. The underlying goal is **obtaining the capability required for a business objective.**

Employment was traditionally the main route, turning missing capability into a vacancy and job description. Alternatives include developing existing staff, reorganizing, consulting, outsourcing, software and AI agents. AI may help companies recruit faster or remove the need for some hiring.

### Why are so many participants needed?

If merely knowing each other existed were enough, hiring would be simple. In reality, at least six costs intervene:

| Cost | Manifestation |
|---|---|
| Defining needs | Employers may not know what they need; descriptions combine vague requirements |
| Information and search | Suitable people may be unknown or not actively looking |
| Capability assessment | Resumes incompletely represent ability; both sides assess fit |
| Trust and persuasion | Employers verify experience; candidates assess credibility |
| Coordination | Interviews, feedback, compensation, offers, exits and onboarding need follow-up |
| Decision failure | Vacancies and poor hires create business and opportunity costs |

Industry roles carry different parts of these costs.

## Why today's industry took this form

HR, platforms, headhunters, RPO and referrals address the same goal with different responsibilities.

| Approach | Main contribution | What employers retain | Typical setting |
|---|---|---|---|
| Internal HR | Coordination, screening, compensation, onboarding and internal ownership | External talent access and final hiring judgement | Ongoing hiring |
| BOSS Zhipin, Liepin and other platforms | Talent traffic, listings, search, recommendations and communication | Defining needs, assessment, persuasion and progression | Relatively standardized, well-supplied roles |
| Headhunting | Proactive search, expertise, relationships and persuasion | Final interviews and decisions | Senior, scarce, urgent or confidential roles |
| RPO | Part or all of the recruiting process | Goals, supplier management and final decisions | Volume hiring or expansion |
| Referrals | Relationships and trust | Limited coverage and scalability | Specialist communities, culture fit and key roles |

### Why recruiting platforms exist

Platforms make information discovery and communication cheaper by bringing many employers and candidates together. They provide markets, traffic and tools rather than owning the entire outcome. Employers retain autonomy but also requirements, assessment and follow-up work.

### Why headhunters exist

Platforms may suffice when talent is abundant and roles standardized. Scarcity, passive candidates, confidentiality and costly mistakes justify proactive service: understand needs, identify targets, make contact, evaluate fit and persuade candidates.

Platforms sell access and tools; headhunters sell expertise and a higher probability of success. The comparison is broader than fees:

> Total hiring cost = channel fees + internal labor + vacancy losses + poor-hire risk.

The same employer can use platforms for ordinary roles, headhunters for senior roles, RPO for volume and referrals for specialist positions. Task complexity and risk differ, not necessarily the employer.

### Why headhunting firms split work into specialized roles

Delivery includes client acquisition, requirements, search, communication, evaluation, interviews, compensation and onboarding. Relationships and judgement require experience; sourcing and organization consume time.

Splitting work among account consultants, managers, delivery consultants, researchers and operators expands senior capacity but creates loss at handoffs, inconsistent criteria and feedback trapped in chats or personal memory.

AI tools initially reduce these coordination costs by organizing clients, requirements, search directions, resume analysis and recommendations. That optimizes the current industry without yet redefining it.

## Which underlying conditions does AI change?

Current roles assume searching, reading, screening, organizing and communicating consume substantial human time. AI changes that assumption unevenly.

| Task | Possible change | Remaining difficulty |
|---|---|---|
| Company research | Rapid information synthesis | Accuracy, freshness and closed platforms |
| Requirements | Extract conditions, contradictions and gaps | Employers may not know the answer |
| Talent search | Understand experience and broaden discovery | Complete, authorized data access |
| Resume screening | Compare many candidates | Resumes do not prove real capability |
| Initial contact | Check interest, location and compensation | Candidate trust |
| Interview assistance | Questions, records and evidence | High-stakes algorithmic judgement |
| Recommendations | Reports and state updates | False certainty in outputs |
| Progression | Scheduling, reminders and offer assistance | Relationships and competing interests |

Search and recordkeeping are easier to compress than judgement, trust, coordination and responsibility. Roles are task bundles: when AI takes many tasks, boundaries shift. A manager and many delivery consultants may become a few experts working with several agents.

### Efficiency and industry restructuring are different

Writing descriptions, screening, preparing questions, summarizing meetings, producing reports and following up are current efficiency improvements.

A deeper change arises when agents maintain goals, use tools, execute, observe and adjust. Then ask whether work still needs a standalone role, whether tasks can recombine, whether the same intermediaries are needed, and whether AI's own execution reduces hiring demand.

Optimization and restructuring can progress at different speeds and coexist for a long time.

## Where recruiting might go

This is a possible path, not a timetable. Different industries and roles may stop at different stages.

### Stage one: everyone has an AI assistant

HR uses AI for descriptions, screening and interviews; headhunters for research, sourcing and recommendations; candidates for resumes, company research and preparation.

Roles remain while output accelerates. More AI-generated listings, resumes and messages may also reduce information credibility and differentiation.

### Stage two: agents take responsibility for workflows

Agents move beyond individual tasks to ongoing ownership of a process. Given business context and criteria, they detect contradictions, maintain candidate profiles, search channels, communicate initially, schedule and adapt from feedback.

People confirm key decisions or handle exceptions. Execution across recruiting, sourcing and delivery roles begins to merge.

### Stage three: employer and personal career agents collaborate directly

A persistent personal agent could maintain experience, capabilities, outcomes, preferences, compensation, location and data-sharing permissions. Employer agents could maintain business needs, criteria, context and hiring outcomes.

With authorization, they match and exchange necessary information directly. Candidates need not browse every listing and repeatedly submit resumes; employers need not wait for applications.

### Stage four: hiring becomes capability acquisition

Instead of asking which role to hire, a company asks which capability is missing for a goal. Answers may include employment, reorganizing, AI, software, outsourcing, consulting or a temporary human–AI team.

The market may expand from matching jobs and resumes to matching goals with capabilities from people, organizations, services, software and AI.

### A possible AI-native recruiting flow

Traditional recruiting starts with a job description and ends at onboarding. A possible alternative extends both ends:

~~~mermaid
flowchart TD
    A[Business objective] --> B[Diagnose missing capability]
    B --> C{Choose acquisition route}
    C --> D[Hire people]
    C --> E[AI or software]
    C --> F[Outsource or consult]
    D --> G[Define outcome-based role]
    G --> H[Discover and match with permission]
    H --> I[Evidence-based evaluation and human decision]
    I --> J[Onboarding and business outcomes]
    J --> B
~~~

The starting point becomes business goals, criteria become outcomes rather than only credentials and keywords, discovery becomes ongoing, matching uses evidence, intent and context, and onboarding is followed by actual business results that improve later decisions.

## How existing roles might change

AI need not eliminate platforms, headhunters or HR. Standardized work may shrink while scarce value recombines.

| Role | Work that may shrink | Value that may remain or grow |
|---|---|---|
| Platforms | Listings, keyword search, manual applications | Networks, verification, authorization, trust and transactions |
| Headhunters | Organization, sourcing, initial screening and reports | Real needs, relationships, expertise, persuasion and outcome responsibility |
| HR | Posting, scheduling, state maintenance and reports | Organizational diagnosis, capability planning, decisions and AI governance |
| Candidates | Repeated forms, searches and standard preparation | Career choice, permissions, judgement and development |

### Platforms may become trusted connections

Networks, accumulated data, relationships and brand trust do not disappear when AI appears. But manual posting and applications may matter less, with value moving toward agents, verification, consent, privacy, reputation and disputes.

Platforms may provide underlying data and trusted connections for independently owned agents. Dominance depends on data, relationships, trust and responsibility.

### Headhunting may become expert service

Research, target lists, search, standard communication and reports are more automatable. Scarce value may concentrate in discovering unstated needs, challenging unreasonable criteria, reaching private networks, assessing complex experience, establishing trust, persuading high-value candidates, coordinating interests and owning results.

Firms may shift from many executing consultants to a few industry experts working with agents.

### HR may focus on organizational capability

Automated posting, screening and scheduling allow more focus on strategic capability needs, whether to hire, develop, buy or automate, organization design, human–AI responsibilities, real-world outcomes and governance of bias, privacy and accountability.

The role may move from completing recruitment to configuring organizational capabilities.

### New infrastructure may be needed

Agent collaboration raises questions of identity, evidence, permission, explainability, appeals and responsibility for leaks or failed hiring.

Future platforms may need verifiable capability profiles, authorization, trust, privacy and accountability—not only jobs and resumes.

## What remains uncertain

Capability does not imply immediate restructuring. Data belongs to platforms, companies and personal accounts; technical comprehension does not grant lawful, sustained access.

Resumes cannot establish all real capability, especially judgement, collaboration and responsibility in complex environments. Career choices and key hires are high-stakes, so both sides may retain final decisions.

AI summaries cannot automatically resolve unclear goals, weak budgets, ambiguous authority or inconsistent criteria. Candidates' decisions also depend on trust in companies, teams and leaders.

Historical data can encode biases about education, age, gender, region and career paths, which AI may disguise as precise scoring. Organizations still need answers about appeals, privacy and responsibility.

Change may be faster in standardized, data-rich roles and slower where local relationships, management and trust dominate. Traditional, assisted and agent-based recruiting may coexist.

## A concluding thought

Today's roles arose from information, search, judgement, trust and coordination costs—not because their division is inherently correct.

AI can reduce some costs and recombine work. It may first improve efficiency, then own fuller workflows, then connect employer and career agents while platforms supply verification, permission, trust and transactions.

Existing roles may retain their scarce contributions in new forms. Whether established platforms, internal agents or new companies lead remains unknown.

> Recruiting may move from jobs, resumes and processes toward goals, capabilities, evidence, authorization and outcomes.

The deeper shift may be from helping a company find a person to helping it obtain the capability needed to achieve a goal.
